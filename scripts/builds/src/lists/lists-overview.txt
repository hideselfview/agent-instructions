builds lists: the lists that builds keeps for a project, in a store outside
every repo.

A project is a set of repos that share lists (bae holds bae and coven). Its
lists are:

  decision stack  what waits on the person: questions, work only they can
                  do, and work that waits until they start it (builds
                  question)
  work queue      what the coordinator and its agents can do (builds item,
                  builds next, builds area)
  decision log    every decision, with who made it and why (builds decision)
  design changes  product design changes (a board or a design-system
                  component, not a software plan) that wait on the person's
                  review or to land (builds design)

No one edits the store directly: a builds command makes every change, and every
command refuses a step that lacks what it needs. Every record (a question, an
item, a decision or a design change) has an id from one counter per project,
which builds prints as #N. Commands take ids as bare numbers
(`builds item take 12`), because an unquoted #12 is a comment in the shell.

The lists hold all the work there is to do: nothing to do is kept anywhere else
(a brief, a handoff, a plan, the decision log or a chat message) unless an item
points at it. Commits, code and repo files never name the lists or their ids.
Neither list holds what is not relevant now, such as a release-time task: open
a GitHub issue for it and take it off the list.


WHO WRITES WHAT

Every write records who wrote it: the name you pass with `--by WHO`, else the
claim of the worktree the command runs in. With neither, builds refuses the
write ("say who you are with --by").

  - The stack holds only what waits on the person, a plan's open questions
    among them. Whoever asks an entry names its effect and what it blocks
    (below). A question that a rule or the code settles is not the person's:
    `builds question settle` closes it, records no decision and stores the
    rule, spec text, earlier decision or code that settles it. A question
    leaves the stack only with the person's answer (a decision) or with such
    a stored reason: a settle, a done, a drop or a defer each needs one.
  - A finding that needs a decision goes on the stack, and whoever found it
    mentions it to the person in one line at most. A finding that needs none
    becomes a queue item, and nobody raises it with the person. When it is
    unclear whether something needs the person, or the work is a new tool or
    product rather than a fix, it goes on the stack, placed first (`builds
    question place`).
  - The queue holds only what agents can do, and `builds next` lists the
    items that are ready. Each item's title is for the person to read.
  - The log holds each answer and each standing choice about how work runs,
    with who decided and why. An answer about a software plan also goes,
    numbered, in the plan's Decisions section.
  - The design session records each design change it draws, and an
    implementing agent records the board edit of a change the person
    decided. The design session approves each design change it records, and
    the person approves one they asked to see first, in its review. The
    decision that settled a board edit is its approval.

A record's origin is the record it came from, and its trail is its chain of
origins from the first record to it. When you make an entry from another
record, name that record with `--origin N`, so the entry's trail shows where
the entry came from. Put an audit's tag (`--tag T`) only on the first entry of
a trail: every later entry's trail has the tag.


EFFECTS AND ORDER OF THE STACK

Every entry names its effect: what the entry affects for the person. Choose
each entry's effect from what its text says. The effects come from the
workflow's definition of critical work (`builds help workflow`, DISPATCH):

  data-loss       data loss: the person could lose music, edits or settings
  bug             a bug the person hits: something in the apps works wrong
  slows-agents    slows the agents: builds or other tooling
  product-design  product design: behavior or a screen the person sees
  cleanup         cleanup or wording: nothing the person notices

builds asks the person the stack's entries from the top, one at a time: first
the entries that someone placed first, in the person's ranked order, then the
rest by how many open items each unblocks, directly or through chains of open
items, then by effect in the order above, then oldest first, and last the
entries that someone placed last, in the same order among themselves. `builds
question place` places an entry first or last, and ranks the entries that
someone placed first in the person's order (DECISION STACK). A session asks
the person one entry at a time, says what it blocks, and talks about that
entry only until the person answers it.
